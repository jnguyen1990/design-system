Notes on direction c

  • densityMost aggressive cut: the page fits one screen. Four cards total versus today's eight. The premise is that a dashboard you check daily should answer one question — am I okay — and make everything else a click.
  • newA sentence, not just numbers. The verdict line states the conclusion in words and the figures back it up. Nothing on the current dashboard tells you whether the numbers are good.
  • densityThree chart cards become one switchable card. Chips pick the view, so the charts cost one card's height instead of three. This is the DS .filter-chip already used in mealplanner.
  • densityTo-dos are chips, not a card each. They collapse to nothing on a clean month, which is the point — an empty state should take zero height, not show "No insights yet".
  • riskTrade-off: this hides the most information. If you like scanning all categories and both charts without clicking, direction a suits you better. It also leans hardest on the verdict logic being right — a wrong headline is worse than no headline.
